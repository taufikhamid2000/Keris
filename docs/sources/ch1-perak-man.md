Confidence: HIGH = official/peer-reviewed or multiple agreeing sources; MEDIUM = single reputable or secondary source; LOW = uncited/blog/conflicting.

## Facts

### 1. Site and location
- Manusia Perak was found in Gua Gunung Runtuh, a limestone cave in the Bukit Kepala Gajah karst hill, Lembah Lenggong, Perak. Bukit Kepala Gajah has about 20 caves; three (Gua Gunung Runtuh, Gua Teluk Kelawar, Gua Kajang) yielded prehistoric burials.
- Source: JWN page "Warisan Arkeologi Lembah Lenggong, Perak" https://www.heritage.gov.my/ms/warisan-arkeologi-lembah-lenggong-perak.html (cave = burial site); Wikipedia Lenggong https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenggong and ipohworld https://www.ipohworld.org/2009/07/28/dem-bones-dem-bones-dem-dry-bones/ (hill name, cave counts; secondary).
- Confidence: HIGH (site/hill); MEDIUM (the "20 caves / 3 burials" count, secondary).

### 2. Discovery: year and who (SOURCES DISAGREE)
- Version A, 1990: Southeast Asian Archaeology blog quoting an exhibition review says found in May 1990. UNESCO's nomination material reportedly says the cave was first excavated in 1990 (via search summary; not read directly). Intensive excavation by the USM team ran 1990-1991.
- Version B, 1991: Ipohworld, Berita Harian and several travel sources say 1991.
- Reconciliation (my reading, an inference): the cave was first excavated/the skeleton exposed in 1990 and excavation continued in 1991; popular sources collapse this to 1991. Not verified against the monograph.
- Who: a team from Universiti Sains Malaysia's archaeology centre (Pusat Penyelidikan Arkeologi Global, USM) led by Zuraina Majid (Prof. Datuk Dr. Zuraina Majid). The 1994 monograph was published by the then Jabatan Muzium dan Antikuiti.
- Sources: https://www.southeastasianarchaeology.com/2006/07/27/perak-man-is-back-in-town-malaysia/ ; https://www.ipohworld.org/2009/07/28/dem-bones-dem-bones-dem-dry-bones/ ; https://search.library.wisc.edu/catalog/999785872202121 (monograph catalogue record).
- Confidence: HIGH that USM team led by Zuraina Majid did it; LOW-MEDIUM on exact year (1990 vs 1991). In-game: say "awal 1990-an" (early 1990s).

### 3. Age and dating method (SOURCES DISAGREE)
- JWN (official): skeleton roughly 10,000 years old ("kira-kira 10,000 tahun"). JWN's English page reportedly says chronometrically dated to about 10,000 years ago.
- Southeast Asian Archaeology blog, ipohworld, many news/travel sources: about 11,000 years; one range given as 10,000-11,000.
- Ipohworld timeline table lists a wide range (11,000-200 BCE) for "Perak man/woman" (a general timeline, not the dating of the skeleton).
- Specific method/laboratory value (e.g. radiocarbon BP figure): NOT VERIFIED. I searched for a "10,120 BP" figure and found no source; do not use it. The method is probably radiocarbon on shell/bone/charcoal but that is an assumption, not confirmed here.
- Sources: https://www.heritage.gov.my/ms/warisan-arkeologi-lembah-lenggong-perak.html (JWN); https://www.southeastasianarchaeology.com/2006/07/27/perak-man-is-back-in-town-malaysia/
- Confidence: HIGH for "about 10,000-11,000 years"; LOW for any single exact figure or method.

### 4. What the skeleton shows
- Almost complete skeleton; JWN calls it the oldest and most complete skeleton found in Southeast Asia (JWN wording: "tertua dan paling lengkap" in Asia Tenggara). Some bones missing (e.g. toes, ribs, parts of the face) per exhibition review.
- Male (sex assessed as male; one source says experts are "not absolutely sure" - treat as probable). Height about 157 cm.
- Buried in a flexed/fetal posture, shallow grave about 0.8-1 m deep (sources: 0.8 m per search summary; about 1 m per exhibition review), oriented roughly east-west across the cave mouth.
- Age at death: sources disagree. About 45 (ipohworld), 40s (exhibition review), 50s (Wikipedia Perak Man section, citing a 2007 Brunei Times article; "citation needed" there). Safe wording: "dalam lingkungan 40-an hingga 50-an".
- A severe tooth infection is given as the likely cause of death by the exhibition review; secondary, not verified. Treat as LOW.
- Sources: https://www.ipohworld.org/2009/07/28/dem-bones-dem-bones-dem-dry-bones/ ; https://www.southeastasianarchaeology.com/2006/07/27/perak-man-is-back-in-town-malaysia/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perak_Man ; peer-reviewed CT/3D-print study: Malaysian Journal of Medical Sciences 2021, doi 10.21315/mjms2021.28.1.1 (via https://zendy.io/title/10.21315/mjms2021.28.1.1; abstract only seen via search, calls it the most complete skeleton in SE Asia).
- Confidence: HIGH (near-complete, male-probable, flexed burial); MEDIUM (157 cm); LOW (cause of death, exact age at death).

### 5. Congenital condition
- JWN states Manusia Perak is the only prehistoric skeleton in the world known born with a congenital deformity named Brachymesophalangia jenis A2 (shortened middle finger bones). Other sources describe the left arm and hand as markedly smaller than the right and a spine curved to the right, possibly from relying on the stronger arm.
- "Only one in the world" is JWN's claim; could not independently verify. Wikipedia hedges ("possibly brachymesophalangia").
- Sources: https://www.heritage.gov.my/ms/warisan-arkeologi-lembah-lenggong-perak.html ; https://www.ipohworld.org/2009/07/28/dem-bones-dem-bones-dem-dry-bones/
- Confidence: HIGH that JWN names the condition and makes the claim; MEDIUM for the arm/spine description; LOW for "only one in the world" as a universal fact.

### 6. Community care (INTERPRETATION, flagged)
- Interpretation: because he lived into middle age with a disabled arm/hand and the burial was carefully made, his community likely supported him. The exhibition review (Southeast Asian Archaeology blog) states this directly; ipohworld notes he reached a "ripe" age for the time. This is an inference by authors, not an excavated fact. Nobody observed care.
- Sources: https://www.southeastasianarchaeology.com/2006/07/27/perak-man-is-back-in-town-malaysia/ ; https://www.ipohworld.org/2009/07/28/dem-bones-dem-bones-dem-dry-bones/
- Confidence: LOW as fact; MEDIUM as a reasonable scholarly interpretation. Label in game: "ditafsirkan / dipercayai" (interpreted / believed).

### 7. Grave goods and burial treatment
- Food offerings: animal bones/meat placed with the body (wild boar, monkey, monitor lizard, deer named by Wikipedia/ipohworld as food deposits) and riverine shells.
- About 10 stone tools with the body; layers of small shells and soil in the grave fill. The review gives 2,878 shells placed on/around the body (single secondary source - LOW-MEDIUM; exact count not verified).
- Search sources describe the layering as food offering, shell layer, more offerings and tools, shell layer, soil.
- No other burials found in that cave (Wikipedia, ipohworld). Whether the "offerings" were ritual food gifts is an interpretation.
- Sources: https://www.southeastasianarchaeology.com/2006/07/27/perak-man-is-back-in-town-malaysia/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perak_Man ; https://www.ipohworld.org/2009/07/28/dem-bones-dem-bones-dem-dry-bones/
- Confidence: MEDIUM (food, shells, ~10 tools); LOW (2,878 count, species list); interpretation (ritual/important person) LOW.

### 8. Tools and way of life
- Stone tools: pebble tools (batu kerikil / pebble tools) for heavy work and flake tools (alat repih) for cutting/scraping; some hammerstones. Wikipedia says about ten stone tools around the body, mostly pebble tools ("citation needed" on that page).
- Hoabinhian attribution (Hoabinhian is the name for this pebble-tool tradition in mainland SE Asia): NOT VERIFIED in my sources for Gua Gunung Runtuh. Do not state.
- Lifestyle: hunter-gatherer (pemburu-pengumpul). Exhibition review lists prey such as wild boar, deer, mousedeer, monkeys, monitor lizards, tortoises and collected riverine shells and plants; this is general site-assemblage interpretation, MEDIUM.
- Wider Lenggong tool record (from UNESCO via search summary): Bukit Bunuh has some of the oldest hand axes found outside Africa, linked to a meteorite impact about 1.83 million years ago; Kota Tampan is a Palaeolithic stone-tool workshop under a Toba ash layer (Toba date: UNESCO text about 70,000 years ago vs. about 74,000 elsewhere - sources disagree).
- Sources: https://www.southeastasianarchaeology.com/2006/07/27/perak-man-is-back-in-town-malaysia/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perak_Man ; https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1396 (not directly fetched; 403) ; https://www.heritage.gov.my/ms/warisan-arkeologi-lembah-lenggong-perak.html (1.83 juta tahun, tapak Paleolitik in-situ).
- Confidence: MEDIUM (pebble/flake tools, hunter-gatherer); LOW (specific species).

### 9. UNESCO listing
- "Archaeological Heritage of the Lenggong Valley" inscribed as a World Heritage Site (cultural), reference 1396, 30 June 2012, 36th session of the World Heritage Committee, St Petersburg; criteria (iii) and (iv). Described as four archaeological sites in two clusters spanning close to 2 million years.
- Sources: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1396 and https://whc.unesco.org/en/decisions/4796 (both returned 403 to my fetch; details from search-result summaries of these pages and Wikipedia Lenggong).
- Confidence: HIGH for 2012 / ref 1396 (widely corroborated); MEDIUM for criteria detail until re-checked on the UNESCO page.
- JWN says the valley's chronometric range is 1.83 million to about 1,000 years ago and is one of the longest archaeological sequences at a single locality.

### 10. Nearby sites
- Kota Tampan (Palaeolithic stone-tool workshop; JWN refers to Tapak Kota Tampan 1987), Bukit Bunuh (very early Palaeolithic), Bukit Jawa, Gua Teluk Kelawar (burials incl. "Perak Woman", discovered 2004 by USM team, about 8,000 years old per ipohworld, uncited - LOW), Gua Kajang, Gua Harimau.
- Management (NST report via search): JWN manages Bukit Bunuh, Kota Tampan, Bukit Jawa and the Archaeological Gallery; Majlis Daerah Lenggong manages the cave sites. A 2019 Star report said several sites, including Gua Gunung Runtuh, were unsafe/neglected. Current access to Gua Gunung Runtuh is therefore uncertain.
- Sources: https://www.heritage.gov.my/ms/warisan-arkeologi-lembah-lenggong-perak.html ; https://www.ipohworld.org/2009/07/28/dem-bones-dem-bones-dem-dry-bones/ ; NST https://www.nst.com.my/node/973453/amp ; The Star https://www.pressreader.com/malaysia/the-star-malaysia/20191221/281492163218601
- Confidence: HIGH (Kota Tampan, Bukit Bunuh part of the listing); MEDIUM-LOW (the rest).

### 11. Current museum and where the skeleton is (PARTLY UNVERIFIED)
- Original skeleton: exhibition review (2006) says the original is housed at the Lenggong museum; a replica was shown in Kuala Lumpur. It went to Japan 7 Sep-24 Nov 1996 (that review).
- Museum name: search sources say it sits at Kota Tampan, opened 2003 as Muzium Arkeologi Lenggong and was renamed "Galeri Arkeologi" (Archaeological Gallery) in 2012; the user's brief says Muzium Arkeologi Lenggong. Which name is current, and whether the original or a replica is now on display, is NOT VERIFIED. Check with JWN / PPAG USM before stating.
- Sources: https://www.southeastasianarchaeology.com/2006/07/27/perak-man-is-back-in-town-malaysia/ ; search-summary of https://www.nst.com.my/node/973453/amp and https://worldheritagesite.org/community/michael-anak-kenyalang/lenggong-valley
- Confidence: MEDIUM (it is held in Lenggong); LOW (current name / original vs replica).

### 12. Tambun rock art (Gua Tambun, Ipoh)
- Painted rock art on the west face of Gunung Panjang in Tambun, Ipoh, overlooking the Kinta Valley. Reported figures: animals (deer, canids), humans, a herd; red pigment probably haematite (The Star, via Wikipedia). Found in 1959 by 2/Lt R. L. Rawlings of 2nd Bn, 6th Gurkha Rifles; reported by J. Matthews in *Man* (1960).
- Age: DISAGREE. Typical Neolithic estimate about 2,000-5,000 years (travel guide), about 3,000-4,000 years (Ipoh Echo 2015), 3000-5000 BC (The Star 2018); one report calls them about 12,000 years old (Malay Mail via Wikipedia), unsupported. James Davisson argues the Neolithic dating rationale is weak and the site may be older. No direct date for the pigment verified.
- Makers: possibly ancestors of Orang Asli (Wikipedia); Davisson suggests Senoi or Semang/Negrito groups. Interpretation only.
- Status: Perak government planned to restrict access in 2018 (Malay Mail); current status unverified.
- IMPORTANT: Tambun rock art is not Perak Man's period or culture and is not part of the UNESCO Lenggong listing. Do not link them as the same people.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tambun_rock_art ; https://www.ipohecho.com.my/2015/08/16/race-to-protect-tambuns-forgotten-ancient-rock-art/ ; https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2018/07/10/perak-government-plans-to-shut-access-to-prehistoric-gua-tambun-rock-painti/1650811 ; https://visitperak.com.my/?p=9757
- Confidence: HIGH (location, 1959 discovery, painted rock art); LOW (any age).

## Safe to state in-game (HIGH confidence only)
- Manusia Perak ialah rangka manusia prasejarah yang ditemui di Gua Gunung Runtuh, Lembah Lenggong, Perak, oleh pasukan arkeologi Universiti Sains Malaysia diketuai Zuraina Majid pada awal 1990-an.
- Usianya kira-kira 10,000 hingga 11,000 tahun (use the range; JWN says about 10,000).
- JWN menyifatkannya sebagai rangka tertua dan paling lengkap di Asia Tenggara, dan mencatat kecacatan kongenital Brachymesophalangia jenis A2.
- Dikebumikan di dalam gua dalam keadaan meniarap/meringkuk (flexed/fetal) with food remains, shells and stone tools nearby (general statement, no counts).
- Hidup sebagai pemburu-pengumpul menggunakan alat batu.
- Lembah Lenggong disenaraikan sebagai Tapak Warisan Dunia UNESCO pada 2012 (ref 1396).
- Tapak berdekatan: Kota Tampan, Bukit Bunuh (Bukit Bunuh among very early tool evidence outside Africa, about 1.83 juta tahun, per UNESCO/JWN).
- Gua Tambun (Ipoh) mempunyai lukisan batu prasejarah, ditemui pada 1959; usianya masih diperdebatkan.

## Dramatisable but must be labelled "Rekaan" (fiction)
- His name, personality, family, language, dialogue, daily routine, specific hunts.
- That a named community member cared for him / fed him because of his arm (the care idea is an interpretation: show as "ditafsirkan", the characters themselves are Rekaan).
- The funeral scene: who dug the grave, rituals, songs, who placed which shells.
- Emotional response to the tooth infection or how he died (cause of death is itself only a secondary-source claim).
- Any story linking him to the Tambun painters, to Orang Asli ancestors, or to any named present-day group.
- Cave life details: fire use, shelter layout, season, weather, what he wore.
- Depicting the moment of the 1990-91 dig with invented dialogue for real people (Zuraina Majid etc.) - use unnamed fictional archaeologists or clearly label.

## Do not claim
- An exact age (e.g. 11,000 or 10,120 BP), a specific radiocarbon lab value, or "dated by X method" without the 1994 monograph.
- A single discovery year as settled (1990 vs 1991) or a named individual "discoverer" of the skeleton beyond the USM team.
- That he is "the only prehistoric skeleton in the world with this condition" as fact (say "JWN menyatakan...").
- That he was a leader/shaman/important person, or that his community cared for him, as fact.
- The 2,878 shells count, the tooth-infection death, species list, or exact age at death as fact.
- That the Hoabinhian label applies (not verified).
- That Tambun paintings are 12,000 years old, that they date to Perak Man's time, or that Perak Man's people painted them.
- That Perak Man is currently on display in the original at a museum named "Muzium Arkeologi Lenggong" (name and original/replica status unverified).
- That Gua Gunung Runtuh is open to visitors.
- That Kota Tampan's Toba date is 74,000 or 70,000 years without noting the disagreement; 30,000 years is an older, superseded figure per one site.
