# Ch3 Fact Sheet: Jalan ke Merdeka (Road to Independence), 1946-1957

Research date: 2026-11-06. Method: web search plus page fetches. Confidence: HIGH = two or more independent sources agree, or a primary/official text; MEDIUM = one decent source, or secondary sources agreeing; LOW = single weak or conflicting source.

Honest limits of this sheet:
- Arkib Negara, Parliament of Malaysia and Perpustakaan Negara pages were NOT directly retrievable in this run. Most facts below rest on Wikipedia (which cites scholarly works), a UK Hansard record, the Wikisource transcription of the 1957 Reid Report, a Malaysian government page (mdbera.gov.my), and press retrospectives. Before shipping, spot-check the HIGH items against Arkib Negara / NewspaperSG (Straits Times, 1 Sept 1957).
- Items marked "from general knowledge, not re-verified" were not confirmed by a fetched source in this run.
- Wikipedia is listed as "Wikipedia (cites: ...)" so you can trace to the scholarly work.

## 2. Safe to state in-game

Each is HIGH unless noted. Wording is paraphrase.

1. The Malayan Union (1 April 1946) put the peninsula under a British governor, reduced the Sultans' powers and offered broad citizenship; many Malays opposed it, and UMNO under Dato' Onn Jaafar led the opposition. (Wikipedia "Malayan Union", citing Albert Lau.)
2. The Union was replaced by the Federation of Malaya on 1 February 1948, restoring the Rulers' position. (Wikipedia "Federation of Malaya".)
3. The Emergency (Darurat) began in mid-June 1948 and was declared over on 31 July 1960. (Wikipedia "Malayan Emergency".)
4. The Alliance of UMNO, MCA and MIC was the coalition that won the 1955 election, 51 of 52 elected federal seats, and Tunku Abdul Rahman became Chief Minister. (1955 election article.)
5. At Baling on 28-29 December 1955 the Tunku, Tan Cheng Lock and David Marshall met Chin Peng and two MCP colleagues; the talks failed. (Baling Talks article.)
6. The January-February 1956 London conference agreed to work toward independence by August 1957. (UK Hansard, 15 Feb 1956.)
7. The Reid Commission had five members: Lord Reid (UK, chair), Sir Ivor Jennings (UK), Sir William McKell (Australia), B. Malik (India), Mr Justice Abdul Hamid (Pakistan). A Canadian nominee withdrew for health reasons. (Wikisource transcription of the 1957 Report. Wikipedia gives the same names with fuller forms for Malik and Abdul Hamid.)
8. The Commission recommended a federal, constitutional-monarchy structure with a Head of State chosen from the Rulers for five years by the Conference of Rulers, and a two-house Parliament. (Wikisource Report; Reid Commission article.)
9. The flag in use at independence was the 1950 Federation of Malaya flag, designed by Mohamed Hamzah; it is the ancestor of today's Jalur Gemilang (renamed 1997). (FMT; mdbera.gov.my.) Description (MEDIUM): red and white stripes, dark blue canton, yellow crescent and 11-point star. Note: FMT says the final flag had 11 stripes; most sources agree on 11 stripes (for 11 states in Malaya's flag), but verify the stripe count before drawing. The count of 11 states is the Federation's nine Malay states plus Penang and Malacca.
10. Merdeka Day events: the Union flag lowered and Malayan flag raised at midnight on the Selangor Club Padang; the formal ceremony at Stadium Merdeka the next morning with the declaration read and "Merdeka!" shouted seven times. (Wikipedia Declaration article; Malay Mail; mdbera.gov.my.)
11. The Duke of Gloucester represented the Queen and handed over the instrument of independence. (Declaration and Stadium articles; consistent across several secondary sources.)
12. Tuanku Abdul Rahman of Negeri Sembilan was the first Yang di-Pertuan Agong. (Several sources.)
13. Tunku Abdul Rahman became the first Prime Minister of independent Malaya. (Common knowledge; not separately fetched in this run, so MEDIUM until you check Arkib Negara.)
14. The national anthem Negaraku was sung at the stadium by a choir on Merdeka morning (Wikipedia, Stadium article; MEDIUM). Note that the Malay Mail piece mentions "God Save the Queen" playing at the midnight lowering.
15. The morning was wet, with a shower that eased for the handover. (Malay Mail 2016 retelling of 1957 coverage; Wong Chun Wai column quoting the Sunday Times, 1 Sept 1957, https://wongchunwai.com/?p=532.) MEDIUM-HIGH.

## 3. Disputed or interpretive

**A. Where the flag lowering happened and when "Merdeka" was chanted.**
- Version 1: Union flag lowered at midnight (30/31 Aug) on the Padang of the Selangor Club; crowd shouts Merdeka seven times there; Tunku then speaks (Wikipedia Declaration article; mdbera.gov.my).
- Version 2: Seven "Merdeka" shouts led by Tunku at Stadium Merdeka in the morning (Wikipedia Stadium article; Malaysian media such as Kosmo/Bernama via search summaries).
- Version 3: Malay Mail 2016 says the Tunku led the cry at midnight and that the BBC reported party youth calling it seven times as the flag was raised.
- Reconciliation: Both events are likely real; sources blur them. The safe line in game is "Merdeka was shouted seven times at the stadium in the morning, led by the Tunku; crowds also cheered Merdeka at the midnight flag change." Treat "who led the midnight shouts" as unresolved.
- Some web pages (FMT, mdbera.gov.my) call the midnight location "Merdeka Square" or "Selangor Club field"; Padang Merdeka is the later name for the Selangor Club Padang. Another page (anyflip) wrongly places the declaration at Padang Pahlawan, Melaka; ignore (that is the 1956 homecoming rally site).

**B. Time of the stadium ceremony.**
- 9:30 am (Wikipedia Declaration article; UTM page via search). One result said "9 am" and an earlier account said 7:30 am. Use "morning (about 9:30)".

**C. Crowd figures.**
- Stadium: "more than 20,000" (Declaration and Stadium articles). Capacity figures vary 20,000-45,000 across Wikipedia sections.
- One source cites 100,000 outside the old Selangor clubhouse around the midnight event; single-source (LOW).

**D. UMNO founding date.**
- 10 May 1946 (Wikipedia); 11 May 1946 in Johor Bahru (Malaysian Bar article); 1 March 1946 or March 1946 congress (other sources). Use "May 1946" with caution, or say "in 1946".

**E. Reid Commission members' full names.**
- Wikipedia: "Hakim B. Malik" and "Sheikh Hakim Halim bin Abdul Hamid". The Report (Wikisource) lists "B. Malik, former Chief Justice of the Allahabad High Court" and "Mr Justice Abdul Hamid, West Pakistan High Court". Prefer the Report's forms. A search query suggested a "Justice Fuad Hassan"; no source supports that.

**F. London talks delegation.**
- Cambridge archive caption lists, seated: Dato' Abdul Razak, Dr Ismail, Col. H.S. Lee, Tunku Abdul Rahman, Dato' Panglima Bukit Gantang, Enche Abdul Aziz, Dato' Mohamed Seth, Dato' Nik Ahmed Kamil. That list mixes Alliance (Tunku, Razak, Ismail, H.S. Lee) and Rulers' representatives (Panglima Bukit Gantang, Abdul Aziz, Mohamed Seth, Nik Ahmed Kamil; the first three are Menteris Besar-level figures). A Malay school resource says the Alliance four were Tunku, Razak, H.S. Lee, Ismail. Tan Cheng Lock is NOT confirmed in London in any source seen. E.E.C. Thuraisingham may have been a non-Alliance adviser (unverified). Mark entire delegation LOW-MEDIUM pending Arkib Negara.
- Sources: https://archivesearch.lib.cam.ac.uk/repositories/2/archival_objects/172243 ; https://premium.pandai.org/note/read/kssm-sj-10-09-01/KSSM-F4-SJ-09-01 ; Hansard 1956 (four representatives each side).

**G. When the first Agong was elected and installed.**
- Elected 31 Aug 1957 (Wikipedia), though its own citation is a Sunday Times item dated 4 Aug 1957 (possible mismatch; one search summary gives 3 Aug 1957). Installed 2 Sep (Wikipedia, Cambridge archive caption) vs 1 or 4 Sep in other sources. Say "early September 1957".

**H. Date the Constitution took effect.**
- Wikipedia: 27 Aug 1957. Keep to "late August 1957" unless verified.

**I. Malay special position, "social contract".**
- Commission (Report) says terms of reference require safeguarding Malay special position and legitimate interests of others; commentators say a 15-year review was suggested, and the final text dropped the limit after Malay objections. "Social contract" as a term is contested (some say it dates from after 1969). Do not present it as an agreed 1957 label.

**J. Flag design contest year.**
- FMT: contest launched 1949, 373 entries. mdbera.gov.my says 1947. Use "1949-1950".

**K. Number of stripes on the 1950 flag.**
- FMT says original design had 11 blue-and-white stripes and final flag has 11 red-and-white ones, and star has 11 points. Many other sources say 11 stripes for 11 states; check an image of the 1957 flag in Arkib Negara before drawing.

**L. Opening of the Stadium.**
- Wikipedia: opening ceremony 30 Aug 1957, Tunku, over 15,000. Single-source (MEDIUM).

## 4. Do not claim

- Do not give exact words of the Tunku's declaration or speech beyond the single word "Merdeka" (the seven-fold shout). Do not invent dialogue for Tunku, Chin Peng, Duke of Gloucester, Tan Cheng Lock, Lord Reid, etc.
- Do not say the Tunku led all seven shouts at midnight, or that the stadium crowd all shouted in unison with a specific word order; sources only say crowd joined.
- Do not say "Union Jack lowered at Stadium Merdeka." Sources put the lowering on the Selangor Club Padang.
- Do not claim Tan Cheng Lock was at the London talks.
- Do not claim "Justice Fuad Hassan" or any sixth Reid member. Do not claim a Canadian member served.
- Do not say there was a referendum on the constitution (none was held).
- Do not claim the Baling Talks produced any agreement or that Chin Peng met the Tunku at the Duke's ceremony or anything similar.
- Do not assert exact minute times (for example "11:58 pm") as certain; they appear in one Wikipedia article without a visible primary citation (MEDIUM).
- Do not claim the exact name of the person who raised the flag on 31 Aug morning (not found). The only named person: En. Tahir Abdul Majid lowered the Union Jack at midnight per mdbera.gov.my (single source, MEDIUM).
- Do not claim attendance totals beyond "more than 20,000" at the stadium.
- Do not claim the phrase "social contract" was used in 1957.
- Do not say Malaysia existed in 1957. It was the Federation of Malaya (Persekutuan Tanah Melayu); Malaysia formed 16 Sept 1963.
- Do not confirm Sir Henry Gurney's assassination date (October 1951, from general knowledge) or the formal 18 June 1948 declaration without checking; the fetched Wikipedia page did not give them.
- Do not use any anecdote about the choir singing Negaraku "for the first time" as a certain fact without checking; it appears in secondary summaries only (MEDIUM).

## 5. Things a bystander could honestly witness (fictional onlooker, no invented real-person quotes)

All scenes below are consistent with sources and can be written without putting words in real people's mouths.

1. **Midnight, 30-31 Aug, Selangor Club Padang, KL.** A large crowd waits in the warm night. The lights go out for about two minutes. When they return, the old flag comes down while the royal anthem plays, and the new red-white-blue-yellow flag climbs the pole to the national anthem. People cheer "Merdeka" together. (Declaration article; mdbera.gov.my; Malay Mail.) HIGH for the sequence; do not say who shouted first.
2. **A wet dawn.** A grey, stormy morning with showers; the clouds thin as the ceremony nears. (Malay Mail; Wong Chun Wai quoting the Sunday Times.) MEDIUM-HIGH.
3. **Streets of people heading for the new Stadium Merdeka**, a stadium finished only ten days earlier (21 Aug) and opened formally the day before (30 Aug). (Stadium article.) MEDIUM.
4. **Stadium morning scene.** Thousands in the stands (more than 20,000), Malay Rulers in ceremonial dress, foreign dignitaries, uniformed bands. A military band plays as the new flag rises; the crowd hears gun salutes (21 rounds) and an azan, then a prayer of thanksgiving. (Declaration article, Stadium article.) HIGH-MEDIUM.
5. **The handover.** A British royal figure (the Duke of Gloucester) hands over a document to the Tunku; the Tunku reads out the declaration; then a roar of "Merdeka" repeated seven times from the field and stands. The onlooker can hear the shout but need not hear the declaration text. HIGH for the structure.
6. **A choir sings the anthem (Negaraku)** in the stadium. (Stadium article; veiled as MEDIUM.)
7. **After the ceremony** people linger for hours; the Tunku walks onto the field to cheers. (Malay Mail retelling.) MEDIUM.
8. **Printed headlines.** Papers announce "MERDEKA!" (Straits Times, printed after 3 am) and the Sunday Mail "The Big Moment"; these can be seen in a kedai or held by a newsboy. (Malay Mail 2016.) MEDIUM.
9. **Late 1955 / early 1956 background scenes** (not witnessed first-hand by a bystander, but plausible): a village near Baling hearing that talks are being held at the English school; crowds queueing to vote on 27 July 1955 (82.84% turnout); a Kampung Baru (new village) in the Emergency with fences and curfew. Keep these generic; avoid claiming specific crowd quotes.
10. **1946 scene.** Malay men wearing white armbands in protest against the Malayan Union and staying away from the governor's installation. (Wikipedia "Malayan Union".) MEDIUM.

## Key figures (one-liners)

- Tunku Abdul Rahman Putra: UMNO leader, Alliance leader, Chief Minister 1955, first Prime Minister of Malaya (the last point MEDIUM).
- Dato' Onn Jaafar: founded UMNO, led anti-Malayan Union movement (Britannica; Malaysian Bar).
- Tan Cheng Lock: MCA president and Alliance founder; at Baling (Baling article). Not confirmed in London.
- V.T. Sambanthan: MIC leader, Alliance founder (Alliance article).
- Tun Abdul Razak and Dr Ismail Abdul Rahman: UMNO members in the 1956 London delegation (Cambridge archive caption, pandai.org). H.S. Lee (MCA) also in the Alliance four.
- Chin Peng: MCP secretary-general; Rashid Maidin and Chen Tien his Baling colleagues.
- David Marshall: Chief Minister of Singapore, at Baling.
- Lord Reid and four co-members: see section 2 item 7.
- Alan Lennox-Boyd: UK Colonial Secretary at the 1956 conference (Hansard).
- Henry, Duke of Gloucester: represented the Queen at the handover (Stadium article, Declaration article).
- Tuanku Abdul Rahman: first Yang di-Pertuan Besar/Agong, died 1 April 1960.
- Mohamed Hamzah: designer of the 1950 flag, Johor architect.
- Stanley Edward Jewkes: PWD director, architect of Stadium Merdeka.
- Sir Edward Gent: first Malayan Union governor, later High Commissioner of the Federation.
- Sir Harold MacMichael: obtained the Rulers' signatures in 1945.

Merdeka (freedom/independence); Persekutuan Tanah Melayu (Federation of Malaya); Kesatuan Malaya / Malayan Union; Perjanjian Persekutuan Tanah Melayu 1948; Darurat (Emergency); Kampung Baru (new village); Parti Perikatan (Alliance); UMNO, MCA, MIC; Rundingan Baling (Baling Talks); Suruhanjaya Reid (Reid Commission); Perlembagaan (constitution); Yang di-Pertuan Agong (Paramount Ruler); Majlis Raja-Raja (Conference of Rulers); Raja-Raja Melayu (Malay Rulers); Perisytiharan Kemerdekaan (Declaration of Independence); Padang Kelab Selangor; Stadium Merdeka; Negaraku (anthem); bendera (flag); nobat (royal orchestra); azan (call to prayer); doa kesyukuran (thanksgiving prayer); Bapa Kemerdekaan (Father of Independence).
