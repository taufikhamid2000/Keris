## 2. Safe to state in-game

Each is supported by at least one fetched source and is low-controversy.

1. Tunku Abdul Rahman publicly proposed a wider association of Malaya, Singapore and the Borneo territories on 27 May 1961 in Singapore. (HIGH)
2. The Cobbold Commission had five members: Lord Cobbold (chair), Wong Pow Nee, Mohammed Ghazali Shafie, Anthony Abell, David Watherston. Two were British-appointed-side and two Malayan-side, with Cobbold chairing (the 2-2 split is a widely repeated description; not verified in a fetched source: MEDIUM). Members: HIGH [WP-COB].
3. Cobbold reported on 1 Aug 1962 in favour of forming Malaysia, with Sabah and Sarawak as equal partners, and described opinion in rough thirds: about a third strongly for, a third wanting conditions, a third split between independence-first and continued British rule. [WP-COB]. HIGH for report conclusion; MEDIUM for exact "thirds" phrasing (Wikipedia quotes it, report text not read).
4. North Borneo's leaders produced a 20-point list and Sarawak an 18-point list setting out the terms they wanted (religion, language, immigration control, representation, native rights, state constitutions). [WP-20], [WP-18]. HIGH that they exist; MEDIUM on dates.
5. The Inter-Governmental Committee (British, Malayan, Sarawak and North Borneo representatives; Lord Lansdowne for Britain, Tun Abdul Razak for Malaya) turned the proposals into constitutional safeguards. [BP-TL] / search summary. MEDIUM.
6. MA63 was signed in London on 9 July 1963. (HIGH) Brunei sent a delegation but did not sign and stayed a British protectorate until 1 Jan 1984. [WP-MA] HIGH.
7. Formation was planned for 31 Aug 1963 but took place on 16 Sep 1963 after Indonesian and Philippine objections and a UN survey in Borneo. [WP-DAY], [WP-MA]. HIGH.
8. The UN team surveyed opinion in North Borneo and Sarawak (no referendum); its report was released 14 Sep 1963 and found the majority favoured joining. Details (about 4,000 people interviewed, 2,200 memoranda) are from [WP-DAY]. HIGH for "survey, not referendum"; MEDIUM for numbers.
9. Singapore left Malaysia on 9 Aug 1965; Parliament voted 126-0 that day. [WP-SEP]. HIGH.
10. Konfrontasi (Indonesia's confrontation policy against Malaysia) ran 1963-1966, mostly small-scale jungle fighting in Borneo, with Commonwealth forces (Britain, Australia, NZ) supporting Malaysia. [WP-KON]. HIGH.
11. Stephen Kalong Ningkan (SNAP founder, first Iban holder) was Sarawak's first Chief Minister from 22 Jul 1963. Donald Stephens (later Tun Fuad Stephens, Kadazandusun leader) was Sabah's first Chief Minister from 16 Sep 1963. [WP-NING], [WP-FUAD]. HIGH.
12. Malaysia Day (16 Sep) has been a national public holiday since 2010. [WP-DAY]. MEDIUM-HIGH.

## 3. Disputed or interpretive (state neutrally, with who argues what)

A. Date shift 31 Aug -> 16 Sep
- Planned originally 1 Jun 1963, later moved to 31 Aug to match Malaya's Merdeka anniversary [WP-DAY] (MEDIUM). Delayed because Indonesia and the Philippines objected and the UN survey could not finish by 31 Aug [WP-DAY], [DFAT/search summary].
- Philippines' stated concern: its claim to Sabah via the Sultanate of Sulu (1878 grant) (search summary; MEDIUM). Indonesia: opposed Malaysia as a British-backed plan (Sukarno's "neo-colonialism" framing is memory, LOW here).
- Manila meetings (Maphilindo, July-Aug 1963) and an agreement that the UN would ascertain Borneo views: from memory; NOT confirmed in fetched sources (LOW as written). Verify before use.
- In East Malaysia many argue 16 Sep, not 31 Aug, is the real national day because 31 Aug marks Malaya's independence [WP-DAY]. Others treat 31 Aug as Hari Kebangsaan. Present both.
- Singapore declared independence on 31 Aug 1963 under Lee Kuan Yew before joining (memory; not confirmed in fetched sources: LOW).

B. Adequacy of consultation
- Supporters: Cobbold and the UN mission found majority support / freely expressed wishes [WP-COB], search summary of UN findings.
- Critics: no Sabahan or Sarawakian sat on Cobbold; about 4,000 people interviewed is questioned as representative; no referendum was held (search summary of Wikipedia/commentary; [WP-DAY]). Critics' names not verified.
- Cobbold privately wrote to PM Macmillan on 21 Jun 1962 that his support depended on Singapore joining; not in the published report [WP-COB] (MEDIUM, single source).

C. Legal status of the 20/18 points
- Per WP-20: some points were put into the constitution to varying degrees; others only accepted orally. Which is which is not specified in the fetched source.
- Original 18-point document has not been located; historian Michael Leigh found no evidence it went to Cobbold; ex-Sarawak AG J.C. Fong called the lists "manifestos" and treated MA63 as the binding text [WP-18].
- Minister Maximus Ongkili argued the 2021 Art. 160(2) change gives them a legal basis since they are appended to the Agreement / Malaysia Act (as reported in [WP-18]).
- Secession (point 7 / no-secession): Jeniri Amir reads it as no right to leave; Abdul Aziz Bari says Parliament has final say, as in 1965; separatist groups argue wording is a recommendation [WP-20].

D. Singapore's exit
- "Expulsion" narrative long taught in Singapore vs later scholarship (Albatross file, cabinet papers) describing a negotiated, coordinated separation (Goh Keng Swee raised it with Razak 15 Jul 1965) [WP-SEP].
- Causes cited: 1964 communal riots, PAP contesting 1964 federal election, "Malaysian Malaysia" campaign, revenue disputes, failed confederation talks Feb 1965. Bellows (1970) says deliberate result of PAP's campaign; Sopiee disagrees (PAP accepted separation to avoid a party split) [WP-SEP].

E. Status of Sabah and Sarawak: "equal partners" vs "states among 13"
- Cobbold spoke of equal partners [WP-COB]. Sabah/Sarawak activists argue they are founding partners, not just two of 13 states; the federal view treats them as states under the Constitution. The 1976 wording of Article 1(2) that listed them as states, and its later amendment (Parliament approved late 2021, effective 2022 per memory), were NOT verified in a fetched source: LOW to MEDIUM. Attribute to "supporters of the amendment" vs "critics" only after reading the Hansard/Act.
- Current issues (2025-26 per WP-MA, whose 2026 entries are not in date order, verify): Kota Kinabalu High Court ruling on 17 Oct 2025 that withholding Sabah's 40% revenue share was unlawful; federal government to review; PM Anwar pledged moves toward one-third of parliamentary seats for East Malaysia; Yang di-Pertuan Agong in Jan 2026 urged return to the Agreement's spirit. MEDIUM-LOW; game should say "debated" and avoid stating outcomes.

F. Ningkan 1966
- Federal side: emergency and removal followed a majority letter from Council Negri members. Ningkan's side: only a no-confidence vote could remove him; High Court initially sided with him; Federal Court (1967) and Privy Council (1968) rejected his challenge [WP-NING]. State neutrally.

## 4. Do not claim

- Do not say a referendum was held in Sabah or Sarawak. None was; the UN survey and the Cobbold hearings were not referendums [WP-DAY].
- Do not say Brunei signed or joined. It did not.
- Do not say 16 Sep 1963 was the date MA63 was signed (9 Jul) or that Malaysia Day = Merdeka (31 Aug 1957 is Malaya's).
- Do not say the Cobbold Commission included a Sabahan or Sarawakian.
- Do not give a single "percent in favour" figure for Cobbold; the report used thirds and a hard core of roughly 20% in Sarawak, "somewhat less" in North Borneo [WP-COB].
- Do not say all 20 or 18 points were enacted, or that none were. Do not give point-by-point legal status without the IGC report / Malaysia Act.
- Do not quote the 18 points as a single located original document; it has not been found [WP-18].
- Do not state Lee Kuan Yew "was expelled" or "chose to leave" as settled fact; both framings exist.
- Do not state Konfrontasi casualty counts, the Manila Accord date, or the embassy burning date unless verified (not confirmed in fetched sources).
- Do not call Sabah's pre-1963 name "Sabah" before 1963 without gloss: it was North Borneo (Borneo Utara); name Sabah was adopted at formation (memory, MEDIUM).
- Do not assert outcomes of ongoing 2025-26 litigation/negotiations.
- Do not put words in the Tunku's or Stephens's mouth; no verbatim quotes were verified.

## 5. Things a bystander could honestly witness

Only concrete, period-appropriate scenes; avoid invented dialogue.

- 27 May 1961, Singapore: journalists at a luncheon hearing the Tunku speak of a wider association; reporters writing notes. (HIGH event; ambiance invented, keep generic.)
- 1962, Borneo: Cobbold Commission members travelling and holding hearings in towns and longhouses, villagers presenting memoranda in groups (method broadly: public hearings plus written memoranda; numbers like 4,000 interviewed are tied to the later UN mission, not Cobbold, in the fetched text; do not attribute to Cobbold).
- 9 Jul 1963, London: signing of the Agreement by delegations from five parties; Brunei delegation present but not signing [WP-MA].
- 22 Jul 1963: Governor Waddell appointing Ningkan Chief Minister in Kuching [WP-NING].
- Mid-Aug to early Sep 1963: UN team members (about ten officials) arriving in Sarawak and North Borneo and splitting into teams to meet people (search summary of UN/DFAT items).
- 16 Sep 1963: formal inauguration of Malaysia; Sabah's first Chief Minister Stephens sworn in [WP-FUAD].
- 9 Aug 1965, about 9:30 am, Kuala Lumpur: Tunku moves the separation bill in the Dewan Rakyat; passes 126-0 without PAP members; announcement over Singapore radio [WP-SEP].
- Konfrontasi: border police posts in Sarawak (Tebedu, Apr 1963) and Commonwealth patrols in Borneo jungle [WP-KON].
- Sept 1966: Sarawak political crisis visible as news: courts, Governor's notice, emergency [WP-NING].

Do not place a bystander at private meetings (Cobbold's letter to Macmillan, Goh-Razak talks, closed IGC sessions).
