# Ch5 Fact Sheet: Malaysia 1969 (Pilihan Raya 10 Mei, Peristiwa 13 Mei, MAGERAN, Rukun Negara, DEB)

Purpose: research input for an educational game (Malay default, English optional). Neutral, non-blaming, school-safe.
Research date: 2026-11-06. Method: web search plus page fetches. Several figures come via Wikipedia, which itself cites primary or scholarly works. Those are flagged "via Wikipedia" and capped at MEDIUM unless a second source agrees.
Confidence: HIGH = two or more independent sources agree, or an official page states it. MEDIUM = one decent source, or sources differ on detail. LOW = contested or unverified.

Key Malay terms: Pilihan Raya Umum (general election); Parlimen digantung (Parliament suspended); Darurat (emergency); Majlis Gerakan Negara / MAGERAN (National Operations Council, NOC); Peristiwa 13 Mei (the 13 May incident); Perintah berkurung (curfew); Rukun Negara / Rukunegara (national principles); Dasar Ekonomi Baru / DEB (New Economic Policy, NEP); Perpaduan negara (national unity); Kaum (ethnic community); Bumiputera.

Popular vote (national figures as given by Wikipedia infobox; East Malaysia parties are inconsistent, do not use):
- Alliance 44.96% (1,077,499 votes) in 1969, down from 58.53% in 1964. Lim Kit Siang's 1974 commentary gives 44.9% and 58.5% (https://bibliotheca.limkitsiang.com/1974/03/30/the-issues-of-the-next-general-elections/). Conf: MEDIUM-HIGH (two sources, one a partisan politician).
- PAS 20.91%, DAP 11.96%, Gerakan 7.47%, PPP 3.37% (Wikipedia, via Nohlen et al.). Conf: MEDIUM.
- Registered voters 3,439,707; turnout 73.59% (Wikipedia). Conf: MEDIUM.
- Peninsula only: Malay opposition share rose from about 15% (1964) to about 25% (1969); non-Malay opposition share stayed about 26%. Opposition seats in the peninsula rose from 8 non-Malay in 1964 to 25. (Malaysian Bar article above.) Conf: MEDIUM.
- Jomo (2004): Alliance kept a reduced majority with under half of the popular vote; roughly half of Malay voters and most non-Malay voters voted against it. Conf: MEDIUM (author's characterisation; scholarly source).
- Alliance lost its two-thirds majority for the first time (Wikipedia; Malaysian Bar). Conf: MEDIUM-HIGH.

State level (Malaysian Bar): Alliance lost Kelantan to PAS and Penang to Gerakan. In Selangor it won 14 of 24 state seats and in Perak 19 of 40, short of a majority in each. Conf: MEDIUM (single source; Perak and Selangor details unconfirmed elsewhere).

## 4. Safe to state in-game (HIGH only)

1. A general election was held on 10 May 1969 (Peninsular Malaysia). The ruling Alliance won fewer votes than before and lost seats; opposition parties (DAP, Gerakan, PAS) gained. (Wikipedia election page; Jomo 2004; Lim Kit Siang.)
2. Alliance had 89 seats in 1964 and 74 after the 1969 elections were completed (including later East Malaysia polling). Opposition parties DAP (13 seats, up from 1) and PAS (12, up from 9) gained.
3. Kuala Lumpur experienced serious communal violence from 13 May 1969; a curfew was imposed and the army was deployed.
4. A national state of emergency was proclaimed on 15 May 1969.
5. The National Operations Council (Majlis Gerakan Negara, MAGERAN) was formed on 15 May 1969, directed by Tun Abdul Razak Hussein, and governed during the emergency period.
6. Parliament did not sit during this period and was restored in February 1971.
7. The Rukun Negara was proclaimed on 31 August 1970, with the five principles: Kepercayaan kepada Tuhan (Belief in God); Kesetiaan kepada Raja dan Negara (Loyalty to King and Country); Keluhuran Perlembagaan (Supremacy of the Constitution); Kedaulatan Undang-undang (Rule of Law); Kesopanan dan Kesusilaan (Courtesy and Morality). Its first stated aim is closer unity among all society. (malaysia.gov.my; the English wording is my translation, as the page is in Malay.)
8. The New Economic Policy (Dasar Ekonomi Baru) began in 1971 with two stated aims: reduce poverty regardless of race, and restructure society so that race is no longer identified with economic function. (Jomo 2004.)
9. The government's official figures for 13 May are 196 dead and 439 injured; these figures are disputed by some researchers. State as "official figures" only.
10. The NOC published an official report on the events in October 1969, titled "The May 13 Tragedy".

## 6. Do not claim

- Do not state any single cause as established fact, including "Malay versus Chinese", "communist plot", "UMNO coup" or "opposition provocation".
- Do not state a death toll other than "official figures: 196 dead, 439 injured" and do not present 600, 800 or 1,000+ as facts.
- Do not give an ethnic breakdown of casualties.
- Do not name individuals as perpetrators or instigators.
- Do not say Parliament reconvened on 22 Sept 1970 (conflicts with other sources), and do not give an exact day in Feb 1971 without confirming against Hansard or Arkib Negara.
- Do not attribute exact East Malaysia election dates or the exact UMNO/MCA/MIC seat split; sources in this research conflict.
- Do not claim the NEP was "created by" the NOC alone or that NEP targets (30% bumiputera equity by 1990) were met; Jomo gives 1.5% in 1969 as the starting baseline and discusses outcomes, so keep to stated aims.
- Do not claim the Rukun Negara text above is an official English translation; the English wording was translated here.
- Do not invent quotes from historical figures.
- Not verified in this pass: Hansard / Dewan Rakyat records, Arkib Negara primary documents, the Election Commission report itself, the NOC report itself. Before release, a human should check these (esp. numbers in section 2).

## 7. Wording guidance (school-safe, non-graphic, non-blaming)

- Use "the 13 May 1969 incident" / "Peristiwa 13 Mei" or "post-election violence". Avoid "race war", "massacre", "pogrom", "killing spree".
- Use passive, outcome-focused phrasing: "communities suffered loss", "families were affected", "Kuala Lumpur was placed under curfew".
- Do not assign blame to any ethnic group, party, or leader. Use "tensions rose after a close election" and "historians disagree about causes".
- Say "official figures state" before any number; add "some researchers believe the real number was higher".
- Keep to feelings of uncertainty, fear and community care; no descriptions of injuries, weapons, or acts of violence.
- Present the policy response as a story of rebuilding: the curfew ended, a council governed temporarily, Parliament returned, the Rukun Negara was written, and the NEP set goals.
- Offer a teacher note: "Scholars still discuss causes; compare at least two views."
- Provide both Malay and English text with the same neutral tone; use Malay terms for official bodies (MAGERAN, Rukun Negara, DEB).
- Add a short reflective prompt at chapter end (for example, "What helps communities trust each other after a crisis?") rather than a quiz on casualty numbers.

## 8. Scenes a fictional bystander could witness without depicting violence

Use a fictional character (for example, a shopkeeper, a bus conductor, a student, a radio listener, a kampung school teacher). No real person named.

1. Election eve, 9 May: posters of many parties on lamp-posts; neighbours of different backgrounds debating over kopi; the bystander notes the town is unusually busy.
2. Polling day, 10 May: a queue at a school hall; ink on fingers; calm, ordinary day.
3. Results night: crowds around radios and a noticeboard; some cheer, some are silent; the bystander senses the mood is tense but no incident occurs.
4. Evening of 13 May: the radio announces a curfew; the bystander closes the shop shutters early and goes home; empty streets, a distant siren, an army lorry passing (no violence shown).
5. Inside the home: neighbours from different communities share rice and water over the fence; elders keep children calm. This is a common theme in recollections (no specific source verified; keep it fictional).
6. Curfew days: a notice taped on a door; curfew hours shorten on the radio; a truck delivers food; the bystander waits for the curfew break to buy groceries.
7. Newspapers briefly absent, then return with stern notices; the bystander reads about the NOC and the Director of Operations, Tun Razak, on the front page.
8. Relief centre: the bystander volunteers handing out blankets or mats at a stadium or school shelter (shelters existed; see Wikipedia 13 May incident on refugees at stadiums). Keep to practical help.
9. 1970: a school assembly or Merdeka day, where students learn the Rukun Negara for the first time; the bystander repeats the five principles.
10. 1971: a radio bulletin about Parliament sitting again; the bystander hears of the new Second Malaysia Plan and a talk on the NEP at the kampung hall: "poverty reduction for all, and a fairer balance in the economy."
