# Ch6 Fact Sheet: February 2020 change of government ("Langkah Sheraton")

Purpose: reference for an educational Malaysian-history game (BM default, EN optional). Facts and dates only, neutral. Language: English with Malay key terms.
Research date: 2026-11-06. Method: web search plus one full-page fetch of Wikipedia (first ~100k of ~337k characters read; its cited references were not individually opened). Bernama's page (bernama.com/en/news.php?id=1916700) returned no article body, so it is not relied on. No Istana Negara / PMO / Parliament primary page was fetched directly; palace and PMO statements are known only through media reports. Every item below is therefore secondary-sourced; confidence reflects that.

Key terms: Langkah Sheraton (Sheraton Move); Perdana Menteri interim / pemangku (interim PM); Yang di-Pertuan Agong (YDPA); Istana Negara; Majlis Presiden Pakatan Harapan; Perikatan Nasional (PN); Pakatan Harapan (PH); Dewan Rakyat; Perlembagaan Persekutuan (Federal Constitution); Perintah Kawalan Pergerakan (PKP / MCO); undi tidak percaya (no-confidence vote); akuan berkanun (statutory declaration).

## 2. Safe to state in-game (HIGH only)

1. On 24 Feb 2020 Tun Dr Mahathir Mohamad submitted his resignation as Prime Minister to the Yang di-Pertuan Agong; the Agong accepted it and appointed him interim Prime Minister.
2. Bersatu withdrew from Pakatan Harapan around the same time, and the Pakatan Harapan government ended.
3. The Agong, Al-Sultan Abdullah Ri'ayatuddin Al-Mustafa Billah Shah, held audiences/interviews with Members of Parliament in the days after (24-28 Feb 2020) to determine who commanded majority confidence.
4. On 29 Feb 2020 Istana Negara announced that the Agong had decided to appoint Tan Sri Muhyiddin Yassin as the eighth Prime Minister, stating he was likely to command a majority.
5. Muhyiddin was sworn in on 1 March 2020 at Istana Negara.
6. The new government was formed by the Perikatan Nasional coalition.
7. The Movement Control Order (PKP) began on 18 March 2020 (context only).
8. Muhyiddin resigned on 16 Aug 2021; Ismail Sabri Yaakob was sworn in as ninth PM on 21 Aug 2021; Anwar Ibrahim was sworn in as tenth PM on 24 Nov 2022.
9. "Langkah Sheraton" is a popular media label that took its name from a gathering at the Sheraton Hotel, Petaling Jaya, on 23 Feb 2020. It is not an official name.

## 3. Contested or characterised differently (who says what)

Reported positions as given by Wikipedia's page (which cites the original statements) and wire reports; no side is endorsed. Confidence MEDIUM that these are accurately attributed; the statements themselves are the persons' own claims.

- Anwar Ibrahim (PKR/PH): said he was told that treachery had been committed by former friends in Bersatu and a small group in PKR. Source: Wikipedia.
- Azmin Ali: said his group acted to protect Mahathir and that a statutory declaration was meant to back Mahathir, not to install a new PM; he in turn said the "traitors" were those trying to displace Mahathir. Source: Wikipedia.
- Mahathir Mohamad: said he had nothing to do with the new coalition arrangement; called the appointment of Muhyiddin illegal (Reuters via Arab News); said Muhyiddin had long sought a government without PH; said a majority was obtained by offering posts. Whether Mahathir was aware of or party to the Sheraton gathering is described in at least one analysis as not fully clear; treat as unresolved.
- Muhyiddin Yassin: said he had no prior intention of becoming PM, denied being a traitor, and said he acted to avoid prolonging the crisis (his first televised address, 1 Mar 2020). Source: Wikipedia.
- Palace: stated the decision rested on Muhyiddin likely commanding majority support and that the process could not be delayed. Source: Reuters via Arab News.
- Legal point: a constitutional academic is reported as saying the proper way to challenge a PM's majority is a no-confidence vote in the Dewan Rakyat. Source: Wikipedia.
- Terminology: supporters of the former PH government commonly use "Langkah Sheraton" with a negative connotation (a move to topple an elected government); supporters of the new government describe it as a lawful change reflecting MPs' support under the Constitution. This characterisation of "who uses the term how" is a general observation from coverage, not a single cited statement; confidence LOW-MEDIUM. Prefer not to attribute it in-game.

## 4. Do not claim

- Do not say the change was "illegal", "unconstitutional", "a coup", "betrayal", "treason", or conversely "fully legitimate and uncontested". Each is a party's characterisation.
- Do not state that Mahathir did or did not plan or know about the Sheraton gathering.
- Do not say who "caused" the fall of the government, or assign motives (greed, principle, etc.).
- Do not state numbers of MPs supporting any candidate as fact (115, 114, 26 + 10 etc.) except attributed claims.
- Do not state the Cabinet announcement date as 9 or 10 March without an official check.
- Do not say the Sheraton gathering itself selected Muhyiddin; the sources describe the gathering and the later appointment as separate steps.
- Do not link the political change causally to the COVID-19 outbreak or the MCO. They overlapped in time only.
- Do not claim the Sheraton hotel meeting was the only meeting that day (Bersatu, UMNO, PAS, GPS also met).

## 5. Wording guidance (neutral, no judgement, no names of private individuals)

- Use "perubahan kerajaan Februari 2020" / "the change of government in February 2020" as the primary descriptor. Mention "Langkah Sheraton" only as "popularly called".
- Template (EN): "In February 2020 the Prime Minister resigned, the coalition government ended, and after the Yang di-Pertuan Agong met Members of Parliament, a new Prime Minister was appointed."
- Template (BM): "Pada Februari 2020, Perdana Menteri meletakkan jawatan dan kerajaan gabungan berakhir. Selepas Yang di-Pertuan Agong menemu duga Ahli Parlimen, seorang Perdana Menteri baharu dilantik."
- Use only the office and public-figure names that appear in sections 1 and 2 (Prime Ministers and the Agong). Do not name MPs, hotel guests or other individuals; "a group of MPs" suffices.
- Prefer verbs "resigned", "was appointed", "was sworn in"; avoid "toppled", "ousted", "seized", "stole", "betrayed".
- Present the label as: "a popular name used by the media and public; it is not an official name".
- Give each side one balanced sentence at most, always attributed ("X said ..."), never as game narrator voice.
- Treat the Agong's role strictly as constitutional procedure (Article 43(2)(a), per press reports on the 2021 appointment; for 2020 the palace's stated basis was likelihood of majority).

## 6. Recommendation for inclusion

Include in a limited form, as a short, factual "constitutional process" event, not as a morality story. Recommended for general players; for school players, include only with the safeguards below, and align with teacher/curriculum review before release.

Safeguards:
1. Use the Section 2 facts only; narrate in the third person with no judgement; no player choice that rewards "siding" with a named politician or party.
2. Frame learning goals around civics: how a Prime Minister is appointed, the Agong's role, majority confidence, and the Dewan Rakyat. Avoid "who was right".
3. Label the term "Langkah Sheraton" as popular and unofficial; offer "perubahan kerajaan 2020" as the neutral title.
4. Keep events from 2021 onwards to one-line dated context; avoid detail on ongoing legal cases or living private individuals.
5. Add a "Sumber" screen listing Istana Negara / PMO / Parliament statements; before shipping, replace secondary links with the official primary texts (Istana Negara, Pejabat Perdana Menteri, Parlimen Malaysia) and have a historian or constitutional-law reviewer sign off.
6. Content-warn that this is recent, still-debated political history; allow educators to disable the chapter.
